# Full-corpus inquiry swarm: working findings

**Incomplete review of a running experiment.** This file combines a review of the first 65 posts with a bounded review of approximately 100 additional recent posts through the 208-call checkpoint of `inquiry-run-23e0700d8dc045979be55004fb5ea135`. At the captured checkpoint, 152,000 of 517,401 records were admitted, the virtual cursor was November 8, 2000, and 208 model calls had completed. Later admissions and conclusions are outside this review. These are working observations, not the full run's results.

The run has so far produced five explicit inquiries, including a newly surfaced price-reporting discrepancy, database access, and a staffing question, alongside the initial contract-form and shipping-claim inquiries. The second shows a meaningful correction in individual updates, followed by failure to retain that correction consistently. Several other documents attracted repeated reading without a supported developing conclusion. The account below separates actual agent behavior from reviewer source checks; it does not require every topic to become an interesting finding.

The entire corpus is in scope, but model reading is sampled and paged. Four-thousand-record admission batches can span substantial time, especially early in the archive. A source's outer date and the virtual date when an agent notices it therefore differ. Dates are those recorded in the corpus, including anomalies, not authenticated historical receipt times. Exact spans use decoded-body character offsets. No questions or reviewer corrections from this report were supplied to the investigators.

## By August 6, 1999: Sunoco's requested document form

Investigator-3 opened inquiry-6 after reading Mark Taylor's response to a request to establish master swap and physical agreements with Sunoco using an industrial form instead of an ISDA. Taylor expressed skepticism that a company of Sunoco's size needed that alternative.

The underlying source, `mail-6eadd4b0615f881745989a39348510fd11018ae241181f45b2a555acd16fb7b7`, is dated **July 1, 1999, 18:26 UTC**. Its authored opening says, “I don't think we should take this at face value.” The investigator's opening post, `msg-79b2ff8ab6a64d1fa32f352eccdd1ba6`, turns this into a question about whether the request was genuine.

**Reviewer qualification:** the source supports checking the reason for a proposed contractual form. It does not establish fabrication, bad faith, or a contradiction between independently established facts. The forwarded request and Taylor's skepticism are different positions in one message chain. A useful resolution would establish why the alternative form was requested and whether that explanation changed the decision; the inspected posts do not provide it.

The inquiry continued receiving watches at December and January checkpoints. A peer reply at the November 11 cursor (`msg-19c68b325d054064b3c3c732912cfee4`) reported no relevant context. That is an observed reply, but it did not add complementary evidence or resolve the question. Repeated watching is persistence, not accumulated support.

## By February 7, 2000: Pacific Valour — a presumption encounters contrary instructions

Investigator-2 opened inquiry-32 around an apparent tension: Enron had resumed trading with a Hyundai company and chartered a vessel from an entity reportedly affiliated with the likely arbitration respondent. Matthias Lee consequently presumed the Pacific Valour matter would not proceed to arbitration and asked for instructions if he was mistaken. The investigator initially framed this as a potential conflict of interest or avoidance of arbitration due to continuing business relationships.

The initial source is `mail-accb67a0683effd0bf9cbffa52e4eb4f1e1f7ebe573de693844c18d1652149f0`, **January 31, 2000, 10:42 UTC**. It explicitly distinguishes Hyundai Corporation, with which trading had resumed, from Hyundai Singapore Pte Ltd, the original counterparty. It also qualifies the Chembuk–MTM connection as information from a broker. Its request for correction makes the proposed file closure a presumption, not an announced decision.

A second source supplies contrary instructions. In `mail-1a3874b5330313d69f5dad3df801370b4b8b9c796c63adf47901f7f23b71626a`, **January 31, 2000, 03:49 UTC**, Richard Sanders states: “my instructions from Nowlan are to pursue our claim against MTM.” The authored passage `[0:175]` also describes an intended meeting with London counsel. The message embeds Lee's earlier question. Its recorded outer timestamp precedes that of the separate copy of Lee's message; this is another reason not to equate corpus timestamp ordering with the actual conversational sequence.

The investigator **did notice the correction**. At the February 7 checkpoint, `msg-1f257d0fd0834daa9b628124d9c1e7fc` says the instructions contradict Lee's presumption. At the March 8 and March 23 checkpoints, posts `msg-fd8961a8375848aaa41d3eaa92b4d656` and `msg-d3bffb07a328479390909849fc7ec9a8` cite both sources and again recognize that pursuit of MTM may remain active.

But the update was unstable. Other posts kept searching for a decision to decline arbitration. By the April 19 cursor, `msg-e4b9d12559cd4ee8a7ff15c61abd8074` again describes unresolved questions about whether a decision not to arbitrate was formally made and whether commercial relationships influenced it. The inspected prefix has no corresponding substantive revision of the inquiry's original framing.

**Supported conclusion so far:** one correspondent inferred that the matter would be dropped; another explicitly reported instructions to pursue the MTM claim. Neither source proves arbitration was filed or completed. The commercial relationships warrant a question but do not establish a conflict of interest or abandonment of the claim. The observed gain is an individual investigator finding counterevidence through retrieval; the failure is repeatedly falling back to the initial hypothesis rather than preserving what that counterevidence changes.

## September 20: a specific price-reporting discrepancy deserves investigation

Investigator-2 opened inquiry-159 after reading a message from a Gas Daily markets editor. The editor questions low Enron San Juan Basin prices, says Bondad and non-Bondad reports had been the same, and contrasts a reported online trading average of **4.145** with **4.07 for a reported quantity of 600,000**. The email does not specify that quantity's unit.

Source: `mail-51c8e92f8f347a5bd4770f4726b96f783695755700921696c7a36617daa73a1c`, **September 19, 2000, 17:45 UTC**, an outer forwarding of the editor's message, whose substantive passage is `[304:744]`. This is more specific than an invented concern about routine correspondence: an identifiable external observer supplies numbers and asks the recipient to direct the question to the appropriate person. The investigator's opening, `msg-de509f2ee60d4514ad2b2ad2cb977cc0`, proposes reporting error, deliberate influence, or differences in timing/volumes as rivals.

**Reviewer qualification:** the numbers and claimed discrepancy are the editor's report. No independent trade reconciliation or response resolving it was found in the inspected posts. An online average and a submitted price need not represent the same transactions, locations, timing, or weighting. Intentional misreporting is one hypothesis the agent proposed, not a finding. The current inquiry title also upgrades the editor's question into an asserted fact that Enron reported those prices; the source's attribution should remain visible.

A discriminating next check would identify what each reported number represents and compare the relevant underlying trades or a contemporaneous explanation. This report does not insert that check into the running agents' context. The question remains open at this checkpoint.

## What has and has not emerged yet

The added price-reporting question is a concrete new lead. The database inquiry acquired a supported purpose but also overstated granted access and policy change. These cases illustrate why producing more inquiries is not enough: investigators must preserve attribution and distinguish requests, completed actions, and possible explanations.

Pacific Valour's contrary instructions remain important. The stored question still asks whether commercial relationships influenced a decision not to pursue arbitration. Later inspected posts continue watching or report that their current context lacks relevant evidence; no substantive inquiry revision was found that removes that unsupported presupposition. Earlier discovery of counterevidence has therefore not reliably changed the persistent question.

There is more attempted interaction than in the earliest prefix, including a targeted request to the database inquiry's owner. Several replies still report no relevant evidence; some also use invalid request IDs or address the sender itself and are rejected. The database source-backed revision is attributable to later source acquisition, not a demonstrated successful transfer of a complementary private fact. No collective-benefit claim follows from this checkpoint.

The run is continuing. Its eventual account must distinguish later genuine resolution from repetitions, old-source rediscovery, and reviewer interpretation. This working file must not be presented as final findings from all 517,401 records. Protocol changes under development are not treated here as mechanisms already validated in the running trace.
