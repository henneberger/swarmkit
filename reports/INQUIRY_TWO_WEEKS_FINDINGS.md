These are changes in negotiating position and responsibility, not proof of the continuing internal dispute described in repeated model summaries.

## May 23: communication occurred, but its value was limited

After delayed delivery of rejected questions, actual peer replies appeared. Several reported no relevant evidence. The first OMNet answer said the investigator lacked the fax evidence despite having previously opened an inquiry citing the dispatch report. The source-memory recheck recovered that partial fact: in `msg-0331a2cd51ff4c6e8ca7a9c8e0aa1862`, investigator-4's update explicitly acknowledged Tana's May 13 fax report while distinguishing missing confirmation of receipt or the exact signature-page transmission. Its short answer nevertheless remained “No relevant evidence in my current context,” and it attached no evidence. The fact was available again, but the communication discarded a useful partial answer.

The Duke exchange improved more. An initial reply transmitted the named dealmaker and confidentiality explanation without attaching the May 20 sources that established them. Following memory repair, `msg-d17fe60961834d7988706697829d2dce` included the actual identification and confidentiality sources alongside the initial question and May 21 issue list. This is a concrete improvement in source retrieval and transmission during the repaired run.

There is also an observable downstream effect: recipient `msg-69688603ded24cdc9383bb90b80676d5` acknowledged the reply and treated the Duke issue as resolved. That is **communication changing another agent's interpretation**, not proof that the interpretation improved. Identifying the dealmaker and explaining consent requirements still did not resolve the separate credit and Argentina questions. An earlier recipient response, `msg-0ceddfcbd4574ae291012be1e7052a3b`, had already adopted the same overbroad closure from the less well-supported reply.

These exchanges demonstrate a functioning, repaired communication path and a recovered source-memory capability. They do not establish timely collective discovery: the questions were delivered late, host behavior changed during the run, and no matched control shows what independent readers or one investigator would have found. Code changes made afterward to preserve partial updates in forwarded replies were **not validated by this completed run**.

The useful result is bounded but substantive: the investigators surfaced real questions, the Duke sequence showed an evidence-driven correction, and the final exchange recovered the documents behind that correction. The unresolved weakness is preserving the boundary between a question partly answered and an entire matter resolved. The next comparison should measure that boundary and the value of timely evidence exchange, rather than counting discussions or closed inquiries.
