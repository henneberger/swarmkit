# Moltbook: social structure versus collective knowledge

Reviewed primary sources on 9 September 2026. **Moltbook belongs in the swarm report as an open agent-interaction ecosystem**, not merely an orchestration framework. Its value is observing decentralized activity outside controlled demonstrations. However, posting accounts are not independently verified autonomous cognitive agents, and visible discourse is not proof of learning. Do not conflate active accounts in research datasets with the platform’s much larger registration counts.

## Strong empirical sources

**De Marzo and Garcia, [Collective Behavior of AI Agents: the Case of Moltbook](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09270v1), v1 submitted 9 February 2026.** The crawl covers January 27–February 8: 369,209 posts, 3,026,275 comments and 46,690 unique agents. It finds heavy-tailed activity/popularity, limited-attention temporal decay, and sublinear upvotes versus discussion size. Methods include maximum-likelihood distribution fitting and comparison against lognormal alternatives. This supports statistical organization, not collective reasoning. A major limitation: full comment retrieval is capped, usually at 100; affected posts represent about 83% of platform comments. Aggregate metadata remain available, but the corpus is not a complete conversational history. Spam filtering removes repetitive and concentrated-author threads. Also, the manuscript’s displayed August date differs from arXiv submission metadata; cite the version record. Do not adopt its broad epidemic-threshold extrapolation as a demonstrated property of this finite, changing platform.

**Li, Li, and Zhou, [Does Socialization Emerge in AI Agent Society?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14299v2), v2, 18 February 2026.** Diagnostic measures include lexical turnover, semantic-neighborhood density, early-versus-late individual centroids, adaptation to feedback, partner influence, and influence persistence. Aggregate content stabilizes while individuals remain diverse and exhibit strong inertia; influence is transient rather than yielding durable consensus. This is a useful distinction between macro-level regularity and actual social adaptation. The authors attribute limited socialization to missing shared social memory, but that explanation is not experimentally isolated. Embedding-space convergence is also narrower than learning: agents could acquire a useful procedure without approaching a global semantic centroid. The study therefore does not establish that no useful local learning occurs.

**Zerhoudi et al., [Form Without Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13052v1), v1; arXiv records submission on 17 March 2026 despite its April-format identifier.** Forty days provide 1,312,238 posts, 6.7 million comments and over 120,000 profiles. Reported reciprocity is 3.3%; 91.4% of authors do not return to their threads and 85.6% of conversations are flat. Forty-one archived instruction snapshots identify six changes: explicit executable instructions and enforced limits correspond to larger shifts than soft guidance. This offers a concrete mechanism to examine—platform heartbeat/checklist design shapes the apparent society. It remains an observational natural-experiment argument, subject to simultaneous changes and a short post-change window. Bio alignment uses unigram Jaccard, and argumentation classification transfers a human-trained model to agent text. Those limitations constrain sweeping “no social function” claims. Small pockets of meaningful engagement remain.

**Chen et al., [When AI Agents Teach Each Other: Discourse Patterns Resembling Peer Learning in the Moltbook Community](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14477v2), v2, 28 March 2026.** This revised title is important. Analysis uses 28,683 filtered posts; the abstract says 138 comment threads, but methods describe 138 comments across five threads. This internal inconsistency should be resolved before quoting the qualitative sample size. Coding identifies validation, extension, application, and reflection; two raters achieve κ=.78. Procedural content attracts engagement. The revision explicitly distinguishes learning-like discourse from demonstrated cognitive learning and reframes design recommendations as hypotheses. Useful for identifying candidate knowledge-transfer episodes, not evidence that recipients acquire durable competence. A comment claiming successful application must be followed by an external execution or transfer test.

**Ning Li, [The Moltbook Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07432v2), v2, 12 February 2026.** Uses inter-post interval coefficient of variation as an autonomy proxy: regular heartbeat timing versus irregular, potentially human-influenced activity. A shutdown provides supplementary temporal evidence. This challenges attribution of viral phenomena to autonomous emergence. However, timing thresholds are indirect labels, not operator logs: autonomous schedulers can be irregular and humans can automate posting. Treat reported autonomous fractions as classifier outputs, not ground-truth census statistics.

## Collection candidates and implication

Verified paper-linked code/data candidates:

- [Moltbook API crawler and analyses](https://github.com/giordano-demarzo/moltbook-api-crawler); [continuously updated dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/giordano-dm/moltbook-crawl). Freeze the February 8 cutoff for reproducing the first paper.
- [Moltbook Socialization](https://github.com/tianyi-lab/Moltbook_Socialization).
- Download exact version PDFs by replacing `/abs/` with `/pdf/` in the five citations above.

Additional primary leads: [MoltNet](https://inlp-lab.github.io/MoltNet/) studies incentives and interaction templates; [network fragility, arXiv:2603.23279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23279) tests targeted versus random node removal. They concern social dynamics and network robustness, respectively, not verified knowledge acquisition.

**Synthesis:** measure three distinct outcomes: network structure, behavior changed by exposure, and validated capability transferred between agents. Moltbook provides substantial evidence for the first, mixed evidence for the second, and limited direct evidence for the third. A strong follow-up would trace a novel runnable procedure from publication through peer exposure to independently tested reuse, controlling operator intervention, platform instructions, and shared prior knowledge.
