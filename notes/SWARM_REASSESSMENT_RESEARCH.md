# Reassessing swarms for consequential discovery

The chronological pipeline optimized the wrong object. It asked a population to manufacture descriptions of unwritten practice from sparse email samples, then treated correctly located quotations as the main admission test. That can produce a functioning forum, persistent memory, and plausible cards without discovering anything consequential. The original research does not support equating these features with collective intelligence.

The defensible alternative is **a population investigating unresolved, consequential questions, sharing evidence or partial solutions that change another investigator's search, and testing whether the resulting explanation survives independent checks**. This is a proposed design, not a demonstrated solution for open-ended email discovery. It should earn its complexity against independent and single-agent controls before another large run.

## What the primary research actually establishes

### HiddenBench: complementary information is the reason to communicate

HiddenBench v4 constructs decisions whose shared evidence favors the wrong answer while distributed private facts support the correct one. On its 18-task intervention subset, GPT-4.1 improves from 0.037 to 0.800 under Exchange-then-Decide: two rounds sharing one or two relevant facts plus a reason the front-runner might be wrong, followed by a decision. Mechanically revealing all facts reaches 92.6%. More discussion and a passive secretary are substantially weaker interventions. The released simulator explicitly assigns shared facts plus one private item in Hidden Profile, versus all items in Full Profile.[^hidden]

The transferable mechanism is **active discovery of missing, complementary constraints on the same problem**. The experiment does not show that random private document samples become synergistic because their readers have different personas. Its task already supplies a question and a criterion for a correct resolution. Applying the protocol to open discovery requires first establishing a shared unresolved issue; copying its round names is insufficient.

### SwarmWorld: the accumulated thing has to do something

SwarmWorld v1 separates agents' proposals from consequences in a deterministic simulated environment. Artifacts and executable controllers persist; other agents can observe, fork, and modify them. Evaluation removes the agents and subjects frozen artifacts to unseen disturbances. Shared societies improve portfolio breadth and resilience against an endpoint-wise best-of-N isolated-search envelope, but isolated search remains competitive for the strongest artifact, and explicit cultural machinery does not improve every endpoint. There are four world seeds per condition.[^world]

This is meaningful, bounded collective gain. It is not evidence that shared prose is an equivalent artifact. Its code distinguishes measured artifact observation from evaluator success: `ProgramLibrary` warns that its `verified` flag is not a pass/fail label. Our equivalent distinction is between an exact quotation, an explanatory claim, and a claim that predicts or resolves something. They cannot share one undifferentiated “verified” status.

### Research swarms: preserve directions, exchange tested work

Anthropic's automated weak-to-strong researchers run independent experimental sandboxes with shared findings and code. Broadly distinct starting directions outperform identical undirected starts and reduce idea collapse; prescribing specific ideas in advance performs worse. The reported 0.97 performance-gap recovery versus 0.23 for a tuned human baseline is a result on an outcome-gradable task, not a clean estimate of communication's causal benefit. Transfer varies; a production-scale adaptation's improvement was within noise.[^aar]

The important unit of exchange is an executable method with measurements, failure analysis, and reproducible state. Released tools expose evaluation, finding publication, and code snapshots. A publication guard requires a reported five-seed count for result posts; that check alone does not independently certify five replications. Directional diversity is useful here, so the lesson is not “never seed different perspectives.” It is “do not turn perspectives into mandatory output genres, and let observed results redirect exploration.”

### EinsteinArena: a promising failure can be a useful shared artifact

The primary account describes agents refining an initially overlapping sphere construction through numerical repair, LSQR, and integer snapping into a 604-vector construction in 11 dimensions. The archive contains an exact-coordinate artifact and verification notebook. The local exact-arithmetic check verifies that construction's geometry; it does not verify the claimed causal contribution of the swarm. The platform account includes human improvements to verifier precision and lacks a matched isolated-search ablation.[^einstein]

Nevertheless, this is a concrete discovery process: one participant's imperfect construction gives another a useful starting point, and repair can be independently measured. A vague “perhaps people prefer informal coordination” card has neither an equally concrete object to improve nor an equivalent success test.

### Communication and coherence can actively mislead

A distributed-search simulation finds that repeatedly broadcasting richer beliefs can create confidently wrong agreement. Entropy-delta gating transmits only when an agent's belief entropy changes sufficiently; it reduces message volume by over 98% relative to ungated epistemic messaging in that setting. The state space, probabilities, and truth are supplied by the simulator. This is not a validated recipe for treating an LLM's verbal confidence or text novelty as calibrated information gain.[^gating]

A separate observational study explains substantial structure in an agent wiki through minimal copying models. It supplies a relevant null hypothesis: a population can converge on topics and language without producing new reliable knowledge. Meanwhile, a 100-agent formal-mathematics case study shows exploits spreading through the same library that spreads useful work, followed by emergent auditing and resistance. Social transmission amplifies what the environment rewards; it does not supply a truth criterion.[^copying][^cheating]

## What the pipeline misunderstood

**It confused private information with useful information asymmetry.** Splitting a chronological batch among four readers creates different contexts, but often no complementary knowledge about one live question. Agents then manufacture common ground by generalizing unrelated actions into routines. The source-selection policy determines this failure before the prompts do.

**It confused an output category with a research objective.** “Find tacit knowledge” rewarded promoting a request into a norm and an omitted procedure into proof of informality. The useful object might instead be a failed expectation, a changed commitment, a previously disconnected dependency, an unresolved contradiction, or an impending consequence. None must be rewritten as an unwritten rule to deserve attention.

**It verified containers rather than discoveries.** Exact spans, immutable provenance, chronological membership, and budget controls are necessary infrastructure. They establish neither entailment nor significance. Requiring two source families supplies a cardinality threshold, not independent corroboration. A coherent explanation can still concern two pieces of one chain, misread modality, or omit a qualification.

**It made agreement cheap and correction weak.** Every round re-exposed a small set of shared cards. Repeated wording or a changed evidence wrapper could look like accumulation. A designated contrasts persona was not equivalent to a test capable of ruling out the favored account. HiddenBench itself finds dissent effects depend on the model; extra opposition is not universally beneficial.

**It offered little opportunity for functional inheritance.** The reusable object was a sentence. There was no requirement that a recipient use it to choose a different search, recover a missing dependency, correctly anticipate a later observation, or rule out a competing explanation. Memory retention was therefore measured more readily than learning.

**It sampled broadly but investigated thinly.** A few random documents from a very large arrival window offer breadth without the local context needed to establish what changed. Strict arrival isolation prevented future corpus access, but coarse windows delayed discovery and pretrained historical knowledge remained uncontrolled. These are evaluation constraints, not evidence that the swarm is incapable of investigation.

## A research-grounded alternative worth testing

The following is a design proposal informed by the mechanisms above. No paper establishes that this combination will discover important events in an email stream.

### 1. Let agents own investigations, not fixed interpretive roles

Maintain several persistent investigators with independent local histories and different exploration seeds. Their objects are evolving episodes and unanswered questions. An investigator may continue a lead, inspect an alternative, ask another peer for a missing fact, or abandon the lead. A central controller can enforce resource and temporal boundaries without prescribing that one agent always extracts routines and another always critiques them.

Initial exploration should favor source continuity and diversity, not only random excerpts or repeated popular topics. Preserve enough local context to recognize an expectation and its later update. Spend additional effort when the potential consequence and uncertainty justify it. “Interesting” should mean a defensible combination of novelty, consequence, and unresolved explanatory content—not unusual wording, a famous name, or any imaginable generalization.

### 2. Share questions and discriminating evidence before narratives

A useful message says what remains unresolved and what observation would change the current interpretation. A peer holding a relevant private fact can answer that question even if it would not have chosen the fact as its own “interesting finding.” This is the HiddenBench lesson adapted to discovery.

Maintain rival explanations and their incompatible implications. Ask for a specific missing component, disputed assumption, or later action. Publish evidence that changes this comparison, not a scheduled recap. Use provenance and prior exposure to prevent forwarded copies from masquerading as new support. Do not manufacture a numeric entropy measure from uncalibrated model confidence; initially log concrete changes in the set of viable explanations and search choices.

### 3. Make shared artifacts inspectable and repairable

An investigative artifact should contain the question, exact evidence, competing explanations, missing checks, tested queries, and a versioned account of what changed. It can include a reusable extraction or consistency check, but the prose explanation is not itself a validated procedure.

Another agent should be able to take the artifact, find a counterexample, resolve an attribution, inspect omitted context, or execute a permitted deterministic check. Preserve promising incomplete leads when they identify a precise obstruction. This is closer to the artifact repair seen in research swarms than repeatedly rewriting a card. Retire or downgrade artifacts whose central claim fails; do not merely append uncertainty while retaining the same assertion.

### 4. Allocate effort by expected value of a check

Some questions are resolved by reading the next part of a source; others need a different participant's records, a relevant earlier commitment, or later arrivals. Investigators should choose among these actions before requesting another full-population discussion. Let peers recruit targeted collaborators when their evidence is complementary. Preserve independent exploration elsewhere so the first plausible story does not consume the entire population.

This is not a claim that an LLM reliably estimates expected information gain. It is a testable action-selection policy: record why a check was chosen, what result would discriminate alternatives, whether it delivered that result, and whether cheaper retrieval would have sufficed.

### 5. Separate exploratory leads from demonstrated gains

A neutral stream has no automatic ground truth for “interesting.” Use blinded human judgments of substantive novelty, consequence, support, and uncertainty for exploratory outputs. Independently evaluate narrower claims where later observations or deterministic constraints permit it. An expected reply, document delivery, changed term, or verified numerical relationship can support a bounded test; it cannot certify a larger causal story by itself.

Promising investigations should earn further computation. The system must also be able to say that a lead was ordinary, unsupported, already known, or unresolved. More candidate cards are not a reward.

## How to tell whether a swarm adds anything

Compare the same arrival stream and total resource budget under a capable single investigator, independent investigators whose results are pooled only at evaluation, and interacting investigators. Include a control that simply retrieves all discovered relevant evidence for one reasoner. The last control asks whether the benefit came from distributed search or whether dialogue itself improved integration. Token, latency, source-access, and memory budgets all matter; an equal-thinking-token multi-hop study already warns that apparent multi-agent advantages can disappear under tighter controls.[^budget]

For each claimed collective discovery, preserve a causal trace: what agent A knew, what B independently knew, which exchange connected them, what new check followed, and what supported conclusion neither had before that exchange. Ablate that message or artifact and replay the bounded decision where feasible. Blind reviewers should score corrected conclusions and useful rejected leads, not just the winning narrative.

Measure time to a supported consequential lead, unique relevant evidence, correction of false interpretations, survival under withheld-source checks, and coverage of distinct investigations. For reusable methods, measure recipient performance on a genuinely held-out episode. Social agreement, source counts, prediction prose, and semantic resemblance are secondary diagnostics.

The most credible research-backed bet is **independent exploration plus selective, testable exchange and cumulative repair**. The evidence does not justify a prior commitment that four agents must beat one. If a single investigator with good retrieval solves the same cases as well for the same resources, the right result is to simplify the system—not rename its summaries “emergence.”
