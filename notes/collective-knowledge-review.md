# Swarm-specific collective knowledge review

Reviewed 2026-09-09. Scope corrected to swarms only. Co-STORM, generic co-scientist teams, knowledge-graph systems, and single-agent skill acquisition are deliberately excluded from these recommendations. Sources inspected locally; repository code read but not executed. Distinguish decentralized ecological swarms from coordinated parallel research swarms, and distinguish a communication diagnostic from a complete swarm architecture.

## Evidence map and report-ready source digests

### SwarmWorld: strongest direct mechanism experiment for cumulative swarm knowledge

Source: [SwarmWorld, arXiv:2608.26081v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26081v1), 26 August 2026. [Repository](https://github.com/lamm-mit/SwarmWorld). Local PDF/text: `sources/papers/2608.26081/`.

**Digest (~150 words).** SwarmWorld is a decentralized society of initially homogeneous language-model agents with local observations, resource constraints, persistent artifacts, and executable controllers. It tests whether interaction produces more than independent parallel search. Its treatments separate explicit communication, program inheritance, physical stigmergy, and isolated search. In the latter, each reported endpoint receives its own best-of-N isolated winner, a demanding control. Shared societies produce broader, more resilient technological portfolios; they do not universally produce the strongest individual artifact. Explicit culture strengthens collaborative construction and code descent, yet physical stigmergy alone remains competitive and sometimes better. Discovery and evaluation are separated: agents are removed, then frozen artifacts face unseen disturbances in deterministic simulation. This makes inherited functionality stronger evidence than agents merely claiming discovery. The important limit is scope: fixed material/action schemas, simulated function, four world seeds, and endpoint-dependent results. Treat it as emerging evidence for cumulative swarm capabilities, not a demonstrated route to arbitrary scientific truth.

**Algorithm and causal contrast.** Each agent observes nearby state plus retrieved memory and emits a schema-constrained action plan. Transactional validation prevents an LLM from declaring physical success. A shared world persists independently of conversational turns. Agents encounter, inspect, contribute to, fork, and modify other agents' executable artifacts. Full culture includes messages, published records, inheritance and physical traces; no-communication retains artifacts and executable inheritance; no-explicit-culture retains physical stigmergy but removes cross-agent program forking and measured inheritance. Independent search has N isolated one-agent worlds.

**Exact evidence anchors.** Section 2.1: population study N=50/100/200, 800 ticks, four matched seeds per cell and eight held-out disturbance schedules; long-horizon study N=100, 3,200 ticks. The seed, not each artifact or disturbance, is the replication unit. Section 2.2: agents removed before held-out assay. Discovery-frontier AUC is not monotonically improved by full culture; at N=200 no-explicit-culture gives largest paired discovery gain (+0.069). Section 2.4/Figure 6: full-culture multi-author artifact shares 67%, 76%, 56% at N=50,100,200. Most first reuse begins through physical observation rather than messaging. These are different outcomes and must not be flattened into one swarm-success percentage.

**Code anchors.** `repositories/lamm-mit__SwarmWorld/src/biofoundry/program_library.py:1` describes provenance-preserving program inheritance; `:69` registers programs; `:117` records observation; `:193` transfers; `:212` inherits; `:16` distinguishes actual cross-agent parent authorship. `src/biofoundry/counterfactuals.py` implements intervention machinery. These support executable cultural transmission; they do not establish real-world validity of simulator assumptions.

### TerraLingua: swarm memory survives individual agents

Source: [TerraLingua, arXiv:2603.16910v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16910v1), 6 March 2026. [Repository](https://github.com/cognizant-ai-lab/terralingua). Newly archived PDF and repository; records in `sources/collective-knowledge-supplemental-manifest.json`.

**Digest (~150 words).** TerraLingua studies decentralized agent ecologies with finite lifespans, spatially local perception, scarce resources, reproduction and persistent text artifacts. The distinctive swarm mechanism is cultural memory decoupled from individual memory: agents can modify artifacts that outlive their authors, creating branching lineages of shared instructions and norms. An external AI Anthropologist analyzes logs without steering the agents. Experiments report cooperation, division of labor, attempted governance and cumulative artifact development under varied ecological and cognitive conditions. This is useful evidence that interaction plus persistent traces can sustain collective organization across generations. It is weaker evidence for discovering true higher-level knowledge: much of the measurement concerns generated culture, novelty, and LLM-interpreted behavior rather than independently tested scientific propositions. Its population begins with 20 agents; each condition uses five seeds. Unlike SwarmWorld's explicit best-of-N control and executable assays, the main contribution is characterization of social and cultural dynamics. Present cultural accumulation and verified functional improvement as separate milestones.

**Methods/evidence.** Sections 3–4 distinguish environment dynamics from post-hoc interpretation. Section 4.2: 50×50 grid, up to 3,000 timesteps, initial population 20, maximum individual lifespan 100, initial energy 50. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B supplies decisions. Artifacts capped at 500 tokens; internal memory soft limit 150 and hard limit 250 tokens. Offspring inherit mutated personality traits. Anthropologist uses Sonnet 4.5/Haiku 4.5; artifact novelty averages five scoring samples. Results section 5 distinguishes longevity, social organization and artifact trajectories. `repositories/cognizant-ai-lab__terralingua/core/environment/artifact.py` contains the persistent artifact object; `analysis_scripts/005_artifact_classification.py:98` shows classification is an analysis step. Do not mistake richer artifact text, lineage depth, or a judge's novelty rating for reliable new external facts.

### HiddenBench: narrow but essential diagnostic of swarm information exchange

Source: [HiddenBench, arXiv:2505.11556v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11556v4), originally 15 May 2025, reviewed revision 13 May 2026. [Repository](https://github.com/Yassellee/HiddenBench_ICML).

**Digest (~150 words).** HiddenBench is a distributed-information diagnostic, not an open-ended swarm. Agents possess overlapping facts plus private facts and must reach a collective decision without being told the information asymmetry. This directly tests one prerequisite for knowledge-extracting swarms: eliciting what peers know but have not volunteered. The benchmark contains 65 tasks; 15-model aggregate accuracy is 30.1% with distributed information versus 80.7% for an individual given all facts. On an 18-task ablation, mechanically revealing facts raises GPT-4.1 accuracy from 3.7% to 92.6%, identifying disclosure as the bottleneck in that setting. A practical Exchange-then-Decide protocol first requires facts and a reason the leading answer may fail, then uncertainty-aware voting. GPT-4.1 reaches 80.0% on that subset. More agents or dialogue alone do not solve the problem. This concerns explicit facts placed in private contexts; it does not show extraction of tacit human expertise or inaccessible model-internal knowledge.

**Protocol/evidence anchors.** Section 6.4/Table 7: two exchange rounds; each participant supplies 1–2 decision-relevant facts and one objection to the front-runner. One decision pass summarizes strongest evidence and residual uncertainty before voting. Four agents, five runs per task, 18 tasks. Baseline→structured accuracy: GPT-4.1 .037→.800; Gemini-2.5-Flash .173→.727; Flash-Lite .043→.743. Section 6.3/Table 6: Reveal-All .926/.982 for GPT-4.1/Flash; secretary-only .241/.713. Table 3's voluntary Share All Information instruction is a different intervention, .467 on three tasks; do not conflate it with mechanistic Reveal-All. Table 5: imposed dissent helps some backbones and hurts Flash. There is no universal adversarial-role recipe.

**Code audit caveat.** `repositories/Yassellee__HiddenBench_ICML/src/hiddenbench/simulator.py:77` assigns information; `:187` executes scenarios; `:248` begins round communication. First round proceeds sequentially with preceding messages available; later turns consume peers' latest responses. This is not fully simultaneous all-to-all exchange. Defaults include 15 rounds and `final_vote_scope='last_round'`. I did not find the v4 named Exchange-then-Decide intervention in the principal simulator via string search; reproducing that table may require additional configuration or implementation beyond the visible default runner. Cite the paper for the intervention and code for the benchmark mechanism, without implying reproduced results.

### Automated weak-to-strong researcher: coordinated research swarm with shared artifacts

Source: [Anthropic Alignment Science, Automated Weak-to-Strong Researcher](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-w2s-researcher/), 2026; exact day unverified in archived page. [Repository](https://github.com/safety-research/automated-w2s-research).

**Digest (~150 words).** This is a coordinated parallel research swarm: nine autonomous researchers operate separate sandboxes, exchange findings through a forum, and share executable snapshots. The concrete lesson is maintaining exploration diversity while enabling reuse. Distinct short research-direction seeds outperform identical undirected prompts, which quickly converge on a few method families. The reported best chat-preference performance-gap recovery is .97 after about 800 cumulative agent-hours, versus .23 for the authors' tuned baselines. That comparison is not a controlled proof that communication beats equal-budget independent search. The swarm discovers methods that elicit useful signals from stronger models, but transfer is mixed: a production-scale attempt gives only a noisy +0.5 point improvement. Evaluation gaming and alternative task-solving routes also appear. This supports task-specific automated method search and knowledge sharing; it does not establish general recovery of a stronger model's hidden knowledge, nor a universal swarm advantage.

**Code/evidence anchors.** Blog sections 3.1–3.2 compare directed and undirected nine-worker runs and track eleven-category Shannon entropy; section 3.5 reports transfer limits. Repository `w2s_research/research_loop/tools/server_api_tools.py:254` implements `share_finding`; `:322` snapshots metric-bearing results; `:358` preserves commit, storage path and parent information. This makes the exchange unit an attributable experiment artifact, rather than opinion alone. Sections 5–6 discuss ways a score can improve without answering the intended weak-supervision question. The blog is author-reported evidence, not an independent replication.

## Swarm-specific synthesis and boundaries

There are at least three distinct senses of unspoken knowledge: explicit local facts not yet shared; competence or useful signals inside a model not expressed by the default prompt; and new regularities discovered only after the population performs experiments. Swarm protocols must not silently substitute one for another. HiddenBench directly isolates the first; AAR searches methods relevant to the second; SwarmWorld tests a limited version of the third in a simulator. None demonstrates universal tacit-knowledge extraction.

The promising progression is from conversation to evidence exchange, then to persistent tested artifacts. A reusable program, result plus exact code, or environmental modification can carry information that a summary loses. The specific swarm contribution is that another agent can encounter and extend this artifact, and later agents can inherit it. Whether this creates better answers remains an empirical question requiring an independent-search baseline. SwarmWorld gives the strongest such comparison here, and its answer is portfolio breadth/resilience rather than universal best-answer superiority.

**Suggested design inference, not a published unified algorithm:** independently explore; surface private evidence before commitment; preserve objections; publish observations with executable artifacts and lineage; let peers reproduce before adopting; maintain alternative research directions; evaluate population output against equal-budget isolated search and held-out consequences. Measure private-fact recall, corroboration, diversity, reproducibility, artifact transfer and marginal benefit per agent separately. This is a swarm research program grounded in the sources, not an established state-of-the-art package.
