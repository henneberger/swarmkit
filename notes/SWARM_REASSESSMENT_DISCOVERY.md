# Reassessing the swarm as an engine for unfolding discovery

The objective was misframed. The user wants interesting things discovered as events unfold: hidden dependencies, changing situations, conflicting explanations, and consequences that are not obvious from individual messages. We substituted a much easier output format: extract a plausible “unwritten rule” from an email, add uncertainty, and put it on a card. Making those cards more carefully qualified would improve their accuracy without fixing the central failure.

The next experiment should ask: **Can interacting investigators explain a developing situation earlier or better than equally resourced investigators who do not communicate?** The answer may be no. A swarm is an experimental treatment, not the product requirement.

This reassessment uses the actual diagnostics, including the interrupted full-v5 run `replay-run-8eed65987d1a4b7d93ef2e0581471394`. It proposes no new paid execution or implementation.

## What failed, beyond prompt wording

We rewarded generalization rather than discovery. A reference to an agreement became an “oral agreement”; an access request became access granted without approval; a scheduling exchange became a rule favoring internal schedules. These are not merely low-interest findings. They manufacture the missing mechanism from absent evidence.

We treated sequence as understanding. Advancing an arrival cursor through the archive does not make a few sampled excerpts an unfolding investigation. The peers repeatedly revisited salient early examples while later evidence arrived without a specific question waiting for it. Random novelty selection can increase document variety while decreasing the chance of completing any explanatory chain.

We treated communication as cumulative knowledge. A peer copying a claim, adding an alternative field, or changing a search query could produce another revision without changing the explanation. Multiple segments of a forwarded chain became candidate eligibility, even though they did not establish repeated behavior. More messages, source IDs, revisions, or consensus are not themselves evidence of collective gain.

We imposed four identities and repeated turns before establishing what the problem needed. “Routines,” “expectations,” “expertise,” and “contrasts” made the same material sound different. Role labels do not create complementary information, competing causal models, or useful interaction. Giving every agent a turn also spends money when the next useful action is a cheap source lookup, waiting for a reply, or stopping an unpromising lead.

Finally, the interface advertised discoveries while exposing a stream of provisional paraphrases. A user should not have to excavate dozens of cards to find the one situation whose explanation actually changed.

## What should count as interesting?

Interesting is a preference-sensitive judgment, not a synonym for unusual wording. Establish a short interest charter with examples of explanations the user values. An initial charter can prioritize consequential coordination failures, surprising dependencies, changing commitments, concealed uncertainty in apparently settled decisions, and mechanisms that connect otherwise separate events. It must not supply known Enron cases or historical answers.

An investigation earns attention when it combines:

- **A real explanatory gap:** the current account cannot explain an observed action, timing, discrepancy, or outcome.
- **Potential consequence:** resolving the gap could change expectations about an outcome, dependency, allocation, or next action.
- **A discriminating next step:** an identifiable source or future event could favor one explanation over another.
- **Evidence beyond a paraphrase:** the proposed explanation connects facts, reconciles a conflict, or changes a prior expectation.

These are admission questions, not four numbers whose multiplication produces scientific objectivity. Record the reasons and compare their predictive usefulness. A commonplace procedure can become interesting when it fails under new conditions; an unusual email can remain irrelevant. An accurately described observation can be far more interesting than a card promoted to candidate-hypothesis status. Do not suppress a striking single event simply because it lacks two source IDs. Open a question, then seek corroboration appropriate to the claim.

Three actual examples illustrate the difference:

**A workaround that can erase itself.** Source `mail-5c3d30a8e011320e3f9916811a9e012816720cb1ae993ac939d74998f1813caf` reports that incomplete upstream meter data becomes zero, downstream staff substitute estimates, and the next upstream refresh overwrites a correction. The forwarded concern connects this to downstream positions and expense visibility. “Staff use informal overrides” is dull. “A repair is nonpersistent because two systems disagree about which value is authoritative; a reported correction can be undone by the next refresh” is a consequential explanatory candidate. Determine which parts are directly reported, whether the mechanism recurs, and whether a durable repair follows. Do not infer misconduct.

**Documents ready, operating arrangement unresolved.** Source `mail-5ba60649499446384f0acfabd1a5459dd5e2eb2ec2d7ea91db107fee7586c315` juxtaposes exchange documents naming Merrill, London’s current use of Paribas/Carr, and finalized legal documents already couriered. “People prefer familiar clearers” invents the outcome. The interesting question is whether legal readiness and operational readiness are being tracked independently, creating a late dependency. The selection remains open until later evidence resolves it.

**A negative example.** Source `mail-2cb44cfd7f1f426d745290d621cea343298ae954d14620a78466533e22985920` shows an external visitor requesting rescheduling around family travel and a host proposing another date around his trips. There is no demonstrated power struggle or consequential change. It should normally expire after a brief inspection, not become an organizational rule.

## Replace extraction with a portfolio of open investigations

The persistent object should be a question with a history, not a declarative rule. Each investigation needs a bounded situation, an observation cutoff, competing explanations, supporting and challenging evidence, unresolved dependencies, and an explicit next observation that would matter. Keep requests, promises, completed actions, and verified outcomes separate.

For the clearing example, alternatives might be: an administrative naming error; reuse of an existing relationship is optional; or a genuine unresolved operational constraint. Those are hypotheses to test, not assertions that every workflow suffers from misalignment. An investigator should be able to say, “This looked consequential, but the next message shows a harmless clerical correction,” and close it successfully.

A low-cost arrival process should connect new messages to active questions through replies, participants, referenced objects, commitments, and source continuity. Novel unrelated material can open a small number of new leads. Do not require a model to reinterpret every arrival. Equally, do not let early favored topics permanently monopolize attention: reserve a bounded exploration share and retire investigations whose expected informational value has fallen.

Before spending a model call, require a proposed action and its purpose: inspect a missing reply, locate a referenced version, test whether a correction persisted, compare an independently authored episode, or challenge an assumed outcome. Estimate the information that could change a decision about the investigation relative to cost. Such estimates are initially heuristics; log them and check whether the actions actually change anything.

“Surprise” should mean a deviation from a recorded expectation. If no expectation existed, call it a new lead rather than a surprising discovery. A prediction needs an outcome, scope, horizon or next-event condition, and a way to recognize resolution. Missing later mail is censored or unresolved evidence, not automatic disconfirmation. A later message repeating the old claim is not necessarily a new outcome.

## What interaction would actually add

A genuine swarm opportunity exists when peers possess complementary evidence or explanatory commitments. One investigator may know the claimed status, another the operational dependency, and a third a conflicting downstream effect. Useful interaction combines these into an explanation none could justify alone. Four agents reading essentially the same excerpts and restating the same theme do not provide that test.

Use temporary responsibilities tied to the investigation: recover a chronology, test a competing mechanism, identify an outcome, or search for a counterexample. Agents should propose and accept bounded tasks, exchange source-backed deltas, and request information from a peer with relevant coverage. Do not mandate four opinions on every email.

A communication message should answer: What new evidence or contradiction do I have? Which open question does it affect? What should the recipient reconsider? Include the source and observation cutoff. Unsupported peer interpretation must remain distinguishable from source evidence throughout relaying.

Consensus is optional. Preserve competing explanations when the evidence does not decide. A successful interaction could narrow uncertainty, expose a missing dependency, or eliminate an attractive theory. A receiver should record a reasoned change or explicitly say that the message added nothing. Avoid a reward for agreement, message volume, or merely citing another peer.

## Demonstrate value with controls and interventions

Start with a small, inspectable evaluation before another broad archive run. Predefine chronological slices and budgets without selecting slices because their eventual outcomes are famous. Keep future documents inaccessible to all model-facing arms. Public-corpus pretraining remains a separate limitation; source-grounded scoring cannot make it disappear.

Compare three primary conditions with the same model and total allowance for tokens, retrieval exposure, and investigation time:

1. **One persistent investigator:** sequentially chooses actions and maintains competing explanations.
2. **Independent investigators plus final aggregation:** parallel search or interpretations, with no intermediate peer exchange.
3. **Interacting investigators:** the same initial allocation, with communication and reciprocal tasks.

Count orchestration, repeated prompt context, retrieval text, and final synthesis in each budget. Equal call counts are not equal cost. Report the cost of unproductive leads as well as the cost of successful ones. In a separate explanation-only comparison, give each arm the identical union of retrieved evidence; this distinguishes better discovery from a mere access advantage.

For promising investigations, intervene on communication. Withhold or delay a particular source-backed message; replay with the same available evidence and budget. Does the explanation fail to form, arrive later, or remain less well supported? Conversely, insert a duplicate or irrelevant message: if confidence rises anyway, the system rewards social repetition. Swap which peer holds a crucial clue to test whether capability depends on information exchange rather than a privileged agent or prompt.

These interventions establish effects within the tested replay, not universal proof of swarm intelligence. Repeat across several investigations and seeds. Do not declare success from one attractive narrative. Private-evidence partitions can test combination, but synthetic partitioning must be reported because it may manufacture a coordination problem absent from normal access.

## Evaluate unfolding explanations, not cards

Have reviewers judge interest and evidential support using only information available at the discovery cutoff. Evaluate predictive outcomes separately after the horizon. This prevents hindsight from rewarding a dramatic story that was unsupported when proposed.

Track useful investigation yield, unsupported-claim rate, information gained per token, and time to a warranted explanatory change. For well-defined predictions, track calibration and resolution; keep unresolved cases visible. Count substantively different mechanisms and conditions, not hypothesis IDs. Audit whether a revision changed a theory because of new evidence, merely reworded it, or silently discarded contrary facts.

Failure criteria should be explicit:

- No additional supported explanation over the matched single-investigator or independent-plus-aggregation controls means no demonstrated swarm benefit.
- Repeated paraphrases, invented negative evidence, and requests treated as outcomes fail regardless of interesting presentation.
- A “revision” without a changed explanation, scope, evidential basis, or test result does not count as progress.
- Claims that depend on messages outside the permitted cutoff invalidate that investigation’s chronological result.
- Confidence or apparent corroboration driven by duplicated messages indicates a communication defect.
- A long run with no useful outcomes should trigger a stop-and-review condition, not a larger run to justify sunk cost.

Set numerical thresholds after a small blinded calibration sample, before the comparative run. They should reflect the user's tolerance for noise and cost rather than fabricated universal standards.

## The experience should make intellectual progress visible

The main view should contain a handful of live questions. For each, show why it matters, the leading explanations, the latest change, what remains unknown, and the next discriminating event. A chronological “theory history” should read: initial expectation; new fact; contradiction; revised explanation; prediction; later resolution or abandonment.

The activity feed belongs underneath this view. A dashboard advancing through hundreds of thousands of emails while surfacing generic procedure cards is activity theater. Show how little was actually inspected and why the selected investigations earned attention. Let the user dismiss a lead as boring, mark a dependency as important, or ask for a different mechanism without rewriting the archive or pretending the preference is ground truth.

The immediate decision is to keep paid execution paused, define the interest charter and review rubric, and manually work a few genuinely open investigation traces alongside the cheapest baseline. Only then test whether a bounded interaction mechanism improves those traces. If it does not, use the simpler investigator. The aim is discovery worth following; the swarm must earn its place.
